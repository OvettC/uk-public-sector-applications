# Civil Service grades and pitching evidence at the right level

Pay ranges change every year and vary by department, profession allowance and location, so take salary from the advert, never from here.

| Grade | Behaviour level | Typical private-sector parallel (rough) | What the panel looks for in evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AA / AO | 1 | Entry level, administrator, first-line support | Own work done well, reliability, customer focus |
| EO | 2 | Team leader, junior analyst/engineer | Small team or workstream, improving how the team works |
| HEO / SEO | 3 | Senior engineer, manager, senior analyst | Owning a service or project, managing people, stakeholder work |
| Grade 7 / Grade 6 | 4 | Head of / senior manager, principal architect, programme manager | Leading teams through other managers, budgets, influencing senior leaders, government-wide view |
| SCS1 Deputy Director | 5 | Director / Head of function in a large organisation | Leading a function (often 50–300+ people), multi-£m budgets, accountability for live services, strategy, external influence |
| SCS2 Director | 6 | Executive director / VP | Departmental strategy, advice to ministers, cross-government leadership |
| SCS3 Director General | 6 | Executive committee / C-suite | Running a business group, board-level governance |

Departments' own grade names differ (e.g. HMRC, DWP band names, NHS Agenda for Change bands, police staff grades). Map them using the advert's line-management and budget figures, not the title.

## DDaT roles

Digital, Data and Technology roles map to the Government Digital and Data Profession Capability Framework (role families such as IT operations, architecture, product and delivery, software development, data, cyber). If an advert names a DDaT role or skill level (awareness, working, practitioner, expert), mirror those terms in technical evidence.

## Pitching the evidence

- Scale is the main signal. Say how many people (direct, indirect, matrixed — be precise about which), how much budget or contract value, how many users or customers, what availability or regulatory bar.
- Matrix leadership counts, but label it honestly. "Led a matrixed team of 12 across five functions" is credible; implying 12 direct reports is not.
- Where the candidate's line-management scale is below the role's, compensate explicitly with accountability scale (contract value, users, revenue or cost under control, number of organisations influenced) and say how they would lead at the larger scale. Panels respect candour more than fog.
- Read the target level's descriptors and make each example visibly do two or three of those things.
