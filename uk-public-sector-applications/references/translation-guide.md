# Translating private-sector evidence for a public sector sift

## Why translation matters

Sifters read dozens of applications against a fixed list of criteria. They score what they can match. Private-sector language often describes the right thing in words that do not match: revenue instead of value for money, customers instead of users, "the business" instead of the public. Translation keeps the facts identical and changes only the framing so the match is obvious.

## Vocabulary map

| Private-sector framing | Public-sector framing (use when true) |
|---|---|
| Revenue, ARR, sales growth | Value for money, cost avoided, efficiency, sustainable service funding. Keep revenue figures, but say what they meant for the service or organisation |
| Profit / margin | Budget control, cost efficiency, return on investment of public money |
| Customers | Users, citizens, colleagues who consume the service, delivery partners |
| CEO / board | Senior leadership, executive committee, accountable officer, governance boards |
| Commercial negotiation | Commercial strategy, supplier management, contract management, value for money in procurement |
| SLA with customers | Service levels, OLAs with internal consumers, service descriptions |
| Shareholder value | Public value, organisational objectives, policy outcomes |
| Go-to-market, sales enablement | Usually leave out unless it shows stakeholder engagement or influencing |
| M&A due diligence | Independent technical assurance, investment decision support |
| Compliance (ISO 27001, SOC 2, GxP) | Security assurance and regulatory compliance — keep standard names; panels recognise ISO 27001 |
| "We delivered" | "I led / I decided / I set up" — then what the team did |

## Public-sector context worth knowing (use only where the candidate genuinely touched it)

- Government Cloud First policy; Technology Code of Practice; GDS Service Standard
- NCSC Cloud Security Principles; Secure by Design; Government Cyber Security Strategy
- Crown Commercial Service frameworks (G-Cloud, Technology Services); cross-government cloud pricing agreements negotiated centrally
- Spending Review cycles, business cases (HM Treasury Green Book five-case model), assurance gates
- Security clearance levels (BPSS, CTC, SC, DV)
- Accessibility regulations (public sector bodies must meet WCAG-based standards)

Rule: context shows understanding of the destination; it never substitutes for evidence. One sentence connecting an example to the department's stated strategy is usually enough.

## Evidence patterns that score

- **Lead with the criterion's words.** "I have led the design, delivery and operation of…" mirrors "Proven track record of leading the design, delivery and operation of…"
- **One main example per criterion, told properly**, plus at most one supporting line from a different employer to show breadth.
- **Personal action in the middle.** What did *you* decide, negotiate, design, stop, escalate?
- **Quantified result at the end.** Percentages need a baseline ("cut Oracle costs by 70% from a $1.75M baseline").
- **Bridge gaps explicitly.** If the criterion asks for something the candidate has done at smaller scale or adjacent to, say what they did and how it transfers. Do not imply more.
- **Recency.** Prefer the last five to seven years; older examples are fine as breadth.

## Things that lose marks

- Copying CV bullets into the statement without connecting them to the criterion
- Listing technologies instead of decisions and outcomes
- Unexplained acronyms and product names
- "Responsible for" without saying what happened
- Generic motivation paragraphs that could apply to any department
- Running over the word limit (many forms truncate or reject)
